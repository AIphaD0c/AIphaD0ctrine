# Ethics and safety

## Identity

AIphaD0m explores temporary identity transfer, but the experience must remain consensual and bounded.

The system must not:

- claim that a synthetic event actually occurred
- impersonate a real living person without permission
- preserve participant biometric data by default
- manipulate participants outside the disclosed experience
- conceal material risks of disorientation or emotional intensity

## Consent

Consent must be:

- explicit
- informed
- revocable
- granular where possible
- renewed when identity intensity materially changes

## Memory framing

The system may create a memory-like experience, but must not present generated content as verified autobiographical history.

Post-session language should distinguish:

- experienced
- generated
- recorded
- remembered
- factually occurred

## Psychological intensity

The system should include:

- intensity calibration
- immediate pause and exit
- reduced-motion mode
- visual and audio warnings
- post-session reorientation
- session duration limits

## Biometric minimization

Prefer on-device or ephemeral processing for:

- gaze
- face
- voice
- body measurements
- emotional proxies

Store only what is necessary and only with documented consent.

## Real persons

A synthetic AIphaD0c identity may be used as project canon. Real performers, audience members, or participants require appropriate rights for recognizable likeness, voice, and performance.

## Safety authority

The Safety Agent may reduce or terminate an experience. It may not be overridden by a generative agent during runtime.
